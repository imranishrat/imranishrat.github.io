RESEARCH PROGRAMME

Transformative Supply Chains

Researching how supply chains can evolve from systems focused primarily on efficiency towards sustainable, resilient, transparent and equitable systems.

Research Perspective

My research examines how supply chains can contribute to economic, social, environmental, cultural and technological transformation. Rather than viewing supply chains solely through the lens of efficiency and operational performance, my research considers their wider role in sustainability, resilience, inclusion, governance and value creation.

Core research question:

How can supply chains transform to generate sustainable, equitable, resilient and socially meaningful outcomes while remaining economically viable?

Research Focus Areas

FA1 — Sustainability & Regeneration

Investigating economic, environmental, social, cultural and technological dimensions of sustainable and regenerative supply chains.

FA2 — Traceability & Transparency

Exploring AI and emerging technologies to improve supply-chain visibility, provenance, traceability, transparency and responsible decision-making.

FA3 — Fair Trade & Governance

Examining governance, ethical sourcing, value distribution, artisan livelihoods and more inclusive supply-chain systems.

Flagship Research Programme

Transformative Cashmere Supply Chains

Challenges and opportunities in the Global South


My flagship research programme examines the transformation of cashmere supply chains, with particular attention to artisan communities and the wider luxury natural-fibre ecosystem.

  • Sustainability and responsible production
  • Artisan livelihoods and wellbeing
  • Cultural heritage and value creation
  • Supply-chain traceability and provenance
  • Artificial intelligence and emerging technologies
  • Fair trade and supply-chain governance
  • Market access and global value chains

Research Context

A particular geographical focus of this programme is the cashmere industry in Kashmir and the wider Global South. The research seeks to understand how traditional knowledge, artisan communities and heritage-based production can be integrated with contemporary sustainability, technology and global market requirements.

Research Methods

My research draws on interdisciplinary approaches combining qualitative and quantitative methods, including case studies, semi-structured interviews, thematic analysis, multi-criteria decision-making, analytical hierarchy processes and emerging computational and artificial-intelligence techniques.
