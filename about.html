About

Dr Imran Ishrat is a Senior Lecturer in Operations and Supply Chain Management at Ara Institute of Canterbury, New Zealand. He holds a PhD in Supply Chain Management from Massey University, an MEng in Engineering Management from the University of Ottawa, Canada, and an MSc in Operations Research from Aligarh Muslim University, India.

His research centres on transformative and sustainable supply chains, with particular emphasis on luxury and natural-fibre industries in the Global South. His current work examines sustainability challenges and opportunities within luxury apparel supply chains, exploring how supply-chain transformation can contribute to environmental, economic and social value. Guided by the United Nations 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, his empirical research addresses issues aligned with SDG 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth), SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure), and SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production).

His broader research interests include process optimisation, operations management and aviation operations management. He is the author of an academic book published by CRC Press and has published in peer-reviewed journals and presented his research at international conferences and academic institutions. His doctoral research was recognised with an Exceptional Doctoral Thesis distinction at Massey University, and his scholarly contributions have received international recognition through an Excellent Paper Presentation Award at the International Conference on Industrial Technology and Management (ICITM), University of Cambridge, UK.

Dr Ishrat supervises postgraduate research and contributes to scholarly activities, including peer review for international journals such as Sustainability. He has extensive tertiary teaching experience in New Zealand and Asia and teaches across Supply Chain Management, Operations Management and Industrial Engineering disciplines.

He is an Incumbent Fellow of the Logistics Institute of New Zealand (LINZ) and an Affiliate of the South Asia Research Institute (SARI) at the Australian National University.
